## Avoiding Snort Detection

This lab has thus far shown how intrusion detection systems (IDSs) like Snort manage and detect alerts. Overall, IDSs play a key role in the defense of a network as they can discover suspicious activity like scans or unauthorized port access. Scans especially can often be loud enough that they can immediately be uncovered. However, IDSs are not omniscient, and hackers, ethical or otherwise, can exploit their faults to bypass detection. A few key methods of doing so include packet fragmentation, spoofing, and time manipulation. 

First, packet fragmentation involves breaking up packets into multiple pieces, and it can be performed through various means. Nmap is the paradigmatic example, and it is a ubiquitous tool in scanning and cybersecurity more generally that can be used for each method of IDS evasion that I will discuss. Take ‘nmap -sS -A -f 172.17.15.12’ as an example. This fragments the packets that are sent with a SYN scan against the target 172.17.15.12. It also offers OS fingerprinting data (Walker, 2022). Fragmenting is useful because it gives the IDS less surface area to detect anything wrong and having smaller packets decreases the likelihood of it being registered as suspicious. One consideration is that fragmentation remains effective as long as you do not flood the network, and this is a point that will be brought up for time manipulation as well. 

Second, spoofing is where you obscure the source of data or a packet. For evading detection, you can fake the source port or the IP address and make the firewall or IDS think that it is from an allowed device or service. Packet builders and scanning tools like Hping, Scapy, Komodia Redirector, and Nmap are all ways of spoofing an IP. However, it is important to not spoof too well, or you will not receive the desired data (Walker, 2022). A part of this is also the IP address decoy. This obfuscates the source through a group of IPs. To do this, you can use more automatic methods like ‘nmap -D RND:10 X.X.X.X’ as well as more granular means, further specifying the decoys (Walker, 2022). Spoofing follows the tenet of pretend that you belong and, in many cases, an IDS will see the fake IP or port as totally legitimate, and you can sneak right through. 

Finally, time manipulation is something that applies to the previous strategies of evasion but also deserves its own discussion. Time manipulation is about adjusting the speed of your scan, and many tools have options to do so. Nmap offers a lot of control over the scan and what packets you want to send, the speed being no exception. To adjust the speed, you use the option ‘-T#’ where the higher the number, the faster the scan is. The range is 0-5, so ‘-T0’ is the slowest scan, and ‘-T5’ is the fastest (Walker, 2022). However, fast scans tend to be louder and get detected easier by IDSs. Thus, time manipulation is vital for slow and methodical scans which are much more likely to slip past. The same principle applies to fragmenting packets, and it is as I said before. If you slowly send the chopped-up packets instead of quickly flooding, evasion is much easier. Ultimately, being patient is one of the greatest traits a hacker can have and allows the highest degree of stealth with the most amount of data. 

In conclusion, IDSs like Snort can be powerful at detecting suspicious signs in a network. However, through various means including packet fragmentation, spoofing, and time manipulation, evasion is possible. Packet fragmentation breaks up packets into separate chunks. Spoofing is where the source of a packet is falsified like a fake IP or port. Lastly, time manipulation is important for slow scans that provide fruitful outcomes. Time and patience will lead to the best results.
